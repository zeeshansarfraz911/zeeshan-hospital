# B2B Cold Outreach Playbook
## The "AI Patient Receptionist" Agent — Pakistani Private Hospitals

**Target buyer:** CEO, COO, Medical Director, Head of Operations, IT Director at Tier-A and Tier-B private hospitals in Punjab & Sindh
**Channel mix:** WhatsApp (primary) + LinkedIn (warm-up)
**Offer:** AI-driven, Urdu/English/Roman-Urdu WhatsApp-native patient receptionist agent
**Hero claims:**
- **Zero upfront installation cost**
- **Guaranteed 35% reduction in missed OPD appointment slots within 90 days** (or first 90 days free)
- **Pay-per-performance model** — only pay for confirmed appointments, not empty promises

---

## 1. Strategy Overview

### 1.1 Why this offer wins in the Pakistani market

| Buyer psychology | How this offer addresses it |
|---|---|
| "Healthcare is relationship business, not tech business" | WhatsApp-native = the channel they already use with family & friends; voice notes for warmth |
| "We get pitched by 5 vendors a week" | Hyper-personalized opener referencing their hospital's specific facts (OPD volume, expansion, recent news) — instantly differentiates from templated outreach |
| "We can't risk patient experience" | 14-day free pilot on ONE department; 90-day performance guarantee; 35% no-show reduction is backed by global benchmarks (Atlas Medical: 14.2% → 4.91% in 3 months = 65% reduction) |
| "Procurement is slow" | Zero upfront cost = no CAPEX approval needed; pay-per-performance; one-line contract |
| "Our patients are low-literacy" | Built for Urdu + English + Roman Urdu, voice-first, picture-supported messages |
| "We're already using WhatsApp informally" | Formalize what they're already doing — receptionist, doctors, even path labs use WhatsApp for booking |
| "ROI is unclear" | Specific, quantified: "PKR 90M/year in recovered OPD revenue at your 200K OPD/year scale" |

### 1.2 The 5-stage conversion funnel

```
STAGE 1 → AWARENESS        (LinkedIn connect + first message)
STAGE 2 → CURIOSITY         (WhatsApp opener with hospital-specific insight)
STAGE 3 → CONSIDERATION     (Case study + audit offer)
STAGE 4 → TRUST             (Live demo + free 14-day pilot on 1 department)
STAGE 5 → CONVERSION        (Performance contract with 90-day guarantee)
```

**Cadence:** 21–30 days from first touch to signed contract
**Average touches:** 7–9 (vs. industry standard 16+)

## 3. Pre-Outreach Research Protocol (the personalization engine)

Before sending a single message, spend 15 minutes on each prospect:

### 3.1 The 7-point research checklist

1. **Hospital website** → find OPD volume, bed count, specialty list, leadership team names
2. **LinkedIn** → find the specific decision-maker; check their activity, posts, tenure, prior companies
3. **Google News / Dawn / Tribune** → any recent expansion, award, controversy, leadership change
4. **Marham.pk / oladoc.com / InstaCare** → patient reviews — look for complaints about "long wait", "no answer on phone", "reception rude", "follow-up booking difficult"
5. **Pakistan's MoFA medical-tourism list / Punjab PHIMC / Sindh insurance panel lists** → confirms their tier and accreditations
6. **Recent peer-reviewed studies** mentioning the hospital (e.g., AKUH has 50+ published studies/year; SKMCH publishes outcomes)
7. **Hospital's social media** → most active platform, recent posts, sentiment

### 3.2 The 3 personalization data points per message

Every outreach message must include at least 3 of these (the more, the higher the response rate):

- A **specific recent fact** about the hospital ("congrats on the 350-bed expansion" or "your SKMCH 2024 annual report mentioned 5 million tests")
- A **specific recent fact** about the person ("saw your post on AI in healthcare last month" or "you've been at LNH since 2018")
- A **specific industry pain point** they've likely encountered ("Pakistan's SMU 2026 audit found 3,576 wrong mobile numbers in one public hospital — your private equivalent is probably similar")
- A **specific peer-operator data point** ("LNH runs 700 beds at 90% occupancy — their front desk handles 2,000+ calls/day; we can automate 60% of those")
- A **mutual connection** or shared event ("we both attended the National EHR Conference 2025 at IHHN")
- A **named, specific doctor** they're connected to ("Dr. Faisal Sultan at SKMCH mentioned you in his 2024 keynote on hospital ops")

### 3.3 The "do not send until you have these" rule

Do not send the WhatsApp opener until you have at least:
- 1 hospital-specific fact
- 1 person-specific fact (LinkedIn activity, role)
- 1 industry pain point they'll recognize

Cold = bad. Cool = warmer. Warm (with research) = high conversion.

### 4.1 The 21-day sequence

| Day | Channel | Action | Goal |

| **Day 18** | LinkedIn | Direct voice note or InMail | Different medium = higher attention |
| **Day 21** | WhatsApp | Breakup message ("closing the file") | Last-chance pattern interrupt |

**Total touches:** 9 (3 LinkedIn + 6 WhatsApp)

### 4.2 Best send times (Pakistan time)

- **LinkedIn:** Tue–Thu, 10:30am–12:00pm or 4:30pm–6:00pm
- **WhatsApp text:** Tue–Thu, 10:00am–12:00pm or 5:00pm–7:00pm (after OPD hours)
- **WhatsApp voice note:** Sat 10:00am (executives check messages on weekends, voice notes stand out)
- **Avoid:** Friday 12:30pm–2:30pm (Jummah prayers), Sunday morning, Monday morning (typically packed with weekend backlog)

### 4.3 Multi-channel etiquette

- **Never** send the same content on LinkedIn and WhatsApp
- **Always** reference the LinkedIn touch on WhatsApp ("Following up on my LinkedIn message…")
- **Always** be ready to move the conversation off-platform to a call once engaged
- **Always** confirm the number before WhatsApping: "Bhai, I have +92 300 XXX XXXX for Dr. Asad — confirm before I send?"

## 8. Objection Handling Playbook

### Objection 1: "Hum already WhatsApp pe bookings lete hain informally"

> "Bilkul — formalize karna hai. Aapke doctors aur receptionists jo ad-hoc WhatsApp pe karte hain woh:
> 1. Hospital ka data nahi capture karte (no analytics, no record)
> 2. Doctor ki availability real-time reflect nahi karti (double-bookings hoti hain)
> 3. Patient ko proper confirmation, reschedule, follow-up reminder nahi milta
>
> Hum wahi informal workflow automate karte hain — but with hospital-grade data, audit trail, and zero double-booking. Aapke existing team ke saath, uske against nahi."

### Objection 2: "Humara patient base literate nahi hai — WhatsApp nahi chalega"

> "Sahi observation — 35-50% Pakistan patients low-literacy hain. Isliye humara agent **voice-first** hai:
> - Voice notes in Urdu (with patient's choice)
> - Picture-based instructions (like Services Hospital ne 2012 mein prove kiya — 3× improvement)
> - One-tap "Haan" / "Nahi" replies
> - Fallback to a human call within 30 seconds if AI confused
>
> Yeh English-typing ke liye design nahi kiya — yeh **amma-ka-phone-use-karne-wale** ke liye design kiya hai."

### Objection 3: "Yeh privacy / HIPAA concern create karega"

> "Valid concern. Hum Pakistan Privacy Act + WHO guidelines follow karte hain:
> - End-to-end encryption on WhatsApp
> - Patient consent captured before any data exchange
> - CNIC number is hashed, not stored plain
> - Audit trail for every interaction
> - Patient can request data deletion anytime
>
> Aur yeh actually safer hai vs. your receptionist writing down a CNIC on a paper register."

### Objection 4: "Humara existing HMS hai (MedicsPK, Switcher, etc.) — yeh us ke saath integrate hoga?"

> "Haan — 14 days mein integrate karte hain. Humara agent kehlata hai [Brand Name] — yeh aapke HMS ka **patient-facing layer** hai, replacement nahi.
>
> - Patient book karta hai (WhatsApp) → booking [Brand Name] ke through aapke HMS mein jaati hai
> - Doctor schedule change karta hai (HMS) → patient ko WhatsApp pe update milta hai
> - Patient lab result query karta hai (WhatsApp) → [Brand Name] HMS se pull karke reply karta hai
>
> Already integrated with: [MedicsPK, Switcher Techno, G4 Technologies, Xenara, in-house HIS]"

### Objection 5: "IT team hamari approval dega — long process"

> "Samajhta hoon. Isliye humara pricing model IT approval ki zaroorat zero kar deta hai:
> - Zero upfront cost
> - Pay-per-performance (no recurring license fee for first 90 days)
> - Cancel anytime
>
> Yeh essentially ek **14-day free trial** hai. IT approval ke baghair bhi start ho sakta hai. Pilot ke baad agar scale karna ho, toh formal IT review process initiate karein."

### Objection 6: "Humne 2 saal pehle ek aur solution try kiya tha — kaam nahi kiya"

> "Kya use kiya tha? [Listen. Most failed pilots in Pakistan were because: (a) they were English-only and patients didn't use them, (b) they required an app download and adoption was 3%, (c) the AI was too rigid and couldn't handle Urdu/Roman Urdu, (d) the vendor had no local presence and integration took 6 months.]
>
> Hum specifically un failure modes ke around design kiye hain:
> - WhatsApp-native (no app download)
> - Urdu + English + Roman Urdu fluent
> - Voice + text
> - Local Pakistan team, 14-day integration
> - 14-day pilot on one department before full rollout"

### Objection 7: "Cost kya hoga?"

> "Three-part model:
> 1. **14-day free pilot** on one department
> 2. **First 90 days: free if we miss the 35% no-show reduction guarantee**. If we hit it, you pay PKR [X] per confirmed appointment.
> 3. **After 90 days**: standard subscription PKR [Y]/month
>
> At [Hospital]'s scale, the recovered revenue (PKR 90M+/year) makes this a 20-50× ROI. Even on conservative math, you save more than you spend."

## 9. The Risk-Reversal & Guarantee Structure (the killer offer)

### 9.1 The "you don't pay if we don't deliver" contract

```
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
PERFORMANCE-BASED CONTRACT
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

PERIOD 1 — PILOT (Days 1–14, FREE)
  • Scope: ONE department (client's choice)
  • Setup: Zero cost, 14-day deployment
  • Deliverable: Baseline audit + live agent operational
  • Client commitment: Provide booking data + access to HMS

PERIOD 2 — GUARANTEE WINDOW (Days 15–104, FREE UNTIL TARGET)
  • Scope: Pilot department + up to 2 more departments
  • Performance target: 35% reduction in missed OPD slots
    vs. baseline (measured over rolling 30-day window)
  • If target HIT: Contract converts to paid from Day 105
  • If target MISSED: First 90 days entirely free — client owes
    nothing, can exit or continue at PKR [reduced rate]

PERIOD 3 — SCALE (Day 105+)
  • Scope: Hospital-wide rollout (all departments, all channels)
  • Pricing: PKR [X]/confirmed appointment OR PKR [Y]/month
    flat fee, whichever suits client
  • Quarterly business reviews with documented outcome data

EARLY TERMINATION
  • Either party can terminate with 30 days' notice
  • No penalties if outside guarantee window

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
```

### 9.2 Why this guarantee is structurally sound (not a marketing gimmick)

- **The 35% baseline is conservative.** Real-world deployments show 45–65% no-show reduction (Atlas Medical: 65%; AI-BAS Istanbul: 10%+; Curogram clients: 10–20% revenue lift). So 35% is a defensible floor.
- **The 90-day measurement window** is long enough for the agent to learn patient behavior, send enough reminders, and demonstrate effect. Shorter (30 days) would risk missing the "learning curve" period.
- **The pilot-department scope** limits client risk to one specialty before hospital-wide scale.
- **The free-exit clause** aligns our incentives with the client's outcomes.

### 9.3 What the agent actually does (the product feature set)

**Core capabilities (Day 1 of pilot):**
- WhatsApp booking confirmation
- 72-hour, 24-hour, 2-hour reminder sequence
- One-tap reschedule
- Cancellation handling
- New vs. follow-up patient triage
- Doctor-availability-aware slot suggestions

**Capabilities unlocked in Period 2 (Days 15–104):**
- No-show recovery: auto-fill cancelled slots from waitlist
- Predictive overbooking: flag high-no-show slots for double-book
- Lab report status query (integrates with hospital LIMS)
- Prescription refill reminders
- Multi-language: English, Urdu, Roman Urdu, picture-based for low-literacy
- Voice note support
- Pre-OPD day instructions ("remember to fast 8 hours before lipid test")

**Capabilities in Period 3 (Day 105+):**
- Post-discharge follow-up automation (the bigger play — see prior problem statement)
- Chronic-disease adherence tracking
- Panel-insurer pre-auth status
- Outbound re-engagement campaigns
- Outcome dashboards for ops and quality teams

## 11. Compliance & Trust Notes

### 11.1 What to NEVER do (Pakistani B2B norms)

- **Never** WhatsApp blast a templated message to 100 prospects
- **Never** use a "+1" or foreign number — get a Pakistani mobile or a recognized business number
- **Never** send PDFs/attachments in the first 3 messages — they're seen as spam
- **Never** use a profile picture of a Western model — use a real Pakistani team member
- **Never** claim "world's first" or "Pakistan's only" without proof
- **Never** ignore cultural norms — "Asalam-o-Alaikum" opens more doors than "Hello"
- **Never** push for the call in message 1 — Pakistani healthcare buyers want value first, ask second

### 11.2 Trust signals to weave in

- SECP-registered company (cite the registration)
- Pakistan-based engineering team (cite names + LinkedIn profiles)
- Named hospital clients (with permission) — "We're working with [name]" beats "we have clients"
- Aga Khan University Hospital or Shaukat Khanum mention = instant credibility (even if not a direct client — "following their published outcomes as benchmarks")
- Pakistan-specific publications (Dawn, Tribune, Profit Magazine, TechJuice)
- National EHR Collaboration Forum (IHHN-led) — "active members"

### 11.3 Data and privacy

- Don't store CNIC in plain text
- Get explicit patient consent before any outbound message (this is required by Pakistan's Privacy of Personal Data Bill drafts and is the global standard)
- Provide opt-out ("Reply STOP to unsubscribe from reminders")
- Audit trail of all patient interactions
- SOC 2 / ISO 27001 in the roadmap (cite as commitment, not current state)
