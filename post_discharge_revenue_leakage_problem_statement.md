# PROBLEM STATEMENT

## The PKR 500–800 Million Leakage: How Missed Follow-Ups and Uncoordinated Prescription Renewals Are Quietly Crippling Pakistan's Top-Tier Private Hospitals

**Sector:** Top-Tier Private Hospitals in Punjab & Sindh
**Subject verticals:** Post-discharge care · Follow-up adherence · Prescription renewal continuity
**Reference benchmark:** Tier-A 350-bed multi-specialty private hospital (e.g., Hameed Latif, Patel, South City tier)
**Audience:** Hospital leadership, medical directors, COO/CFO, heads of pharmacy, quality & patient experience

---

## 1. Executive Summary (TL;DR)

A representative top-tier private hospital in Pakistan loses an estimated **PKR 500–800 million per year** — roughly **15–25% of annual revenue** — to a system of failures that starts the moment a patient is discharged. The losses are split across five measurable layers:

| Layer | Annual leakage (PKR) | Root cause |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Direct missed follow-up OPD revenue | **90 M** | 20% follow-up no-show rate × 100K scheduled visits |
| 2. Avoidable readmission opportunity cost | **162 M** | 15.3% 30-day readmission rate; 83% of those missed follow-ups |
| 3. Uncoordinated prescription renewal | **146 M** | 55–60% chronic-disease non-adherence × PKR 487M pharmacy revenue |
| 4. Lost patient lifetime value (amortized) | **214 M** | 30% of discharged patients have poor experience; 40% don't return/refer |
| 5. Staff & operational waste | **27 M** | 25–50 min staff time per missed appointment × 20K misses |
| **Total annual leakage** | **~PKR 639 M** | **Range: PKR 500–800 M** |

Across Pakistan's 50+ Tier-A and Tier-B private hospitals, the **industry-wide annual leakage is conservatively PKR 25–40 billion** — a sum larger than the entire private hospital sector's annual capex in most years.

The problem is **structural, not clinical**: Pakistan's top hospitals have invested heavily in admission, diagnosis, and surgery — but treat the moment of discharge as the **end** of the episode, not the start of a 30- to 90-day care continuation. The peer-reviewed evidence base is unambiguous: discharge processes in Pakistan are **fragmented, manually documented, and reactive**, not coordinated, automated, and proactive.

> **The single biggest unlock is the one nobody is building: an integrated post-discharge care layer that bridges discharge to the next 90 days of life — automated follow-up scheduling, multi-channel reminders, prescription refill coordination, chronic-disease adherence tracking, and outcome capture — built on a unified patient identity.**

## 2. The Problem in Detail

### 2.1 The "discharge cliff"

In a typical top-tier Pakistani private hospital, the patient journey looks like this:

```
ADMISSION ──► DIAGNOSIS ──► TREATMENT ──► DISCHARGE ───────────►  ???
                                              │
                                              │  ←─ Manual discharge summary
                                              │  ←─ Hand-written prescription
                                              │  ←─ Verbal follow-up instruction
                                              │  ←─ "Come back in 2 weeks"
                                              │
                                              ▼
                                          PATIENT GOES HOME
                                          (often rural, low-literacy, no support)
                                              │
                                              │  ←─ No proactive contact in 7-14 days
                                              │  ←─ No medication adherence check
                                              │  ←─ No red-flag symptom monitoring
                                              │  ←─ No appointment confirmation
                                              │
                                              ▼
                                       30-DAY READMISSION
                                       or chronic deterioration
                                       or silent loss to follow-up
```

The **AKUH Hospital Discharge page** (the only one in Pakistan that publishes this publicly) describes the post-discharge instructions as: "Our staff will schedule a follow up appointment for you to return to see the doctor in the clinic if necessary" and "You may be provided with relevant instructions and information leaflets (e.g. diet, medications, wound care) for you to follow at home." The word "may" appears three times. There is no mention of proactive outreach, no medication-adherence programme, no remote-monitoring, no care-coordination contact.

This is not an AKUH-specific failure. It is a **sector-wide pattern** confirmed by every peer-reviewed study in the country.

### 2.2 What "managing follow-up" actually looks like today

At most Tier-A private hospitals, "follow-up management" means:
- A paper slip with a date written on it, handed to the patient at discharge.
- A reminder phone call (if the front desk remembers) 1–2 days before the appointment.
- No systematic check whether the patient took their medication, understood their discharge instructions, or had a red-flag symptom.
- No tracking of who actually showed up vs. who didn't.
- No automated re-scheduling for the missed patients.
- No data on follow-up adherence rate at the hospital level.

At the **two hospitals in Pakistan with fully paperless EMR (Shaukat Khanum + Indus)**, the data is captured — but it isn't yet driving **proactive outbound action**. The EMR is a system of record, not a system of engagement.

## 3. Evidence Base (Pakistan-specific, peer-reviewed)

### 3.1 Readmission and missed follow-up — the AKUH benchmark

A retrospective study at **Aga Khan University Hospital (2018–2019)** of 491 randomly selected Internal Medicine discharges found:
- **15.3% (75 patients) were readmitted within 30 days** of discharge.
- **83% of readmitted patients had missed their scheduled follow-up appointment.**
- **37.3% of readmitted patients re-presented within 7 days of discharge** — i.e., before their scheduled follow-up, indicating the patient deteriorated at home with no monitoring.
- Most common readmission causes: **persistence of symptoms (43%)** and **nosocomial infection (29%)** — both potentially modifiable with structured post-discharge care.
- **Mortality in the readmitted cohort: 12%.**

This is the strongest single-site evidence in Pakistan that **missed follow-ups drive avoidable readmissions**.

### 3.2 Discharge documentation — a sector-wide quality crisis

**Mardan Medical Complex audit (2024, PMC):** Of 105 discharge summaries audited:
- **Follow-up appointments specified in only 71% of cases** (in some sub-areas, as low as 17%).
- **Medication dose missing in 61%**, route missing in 28%, duration missing in 20%.
- Patient contact details **absent in 100%** of summaries.
- 73% missing clinical summary; 87% missing procedural details.

**Holy Family Hospital Rawalpindi QIP (PMC, 2023):** Baseline audit of 150 discharge summaries:
- Allergies recorded in only **3%** of summaries.
- Patient's date of birth in only **2%**.
- Past medical history in **52.7%**.
- Follow-up plan written clearly in **65%** (improved to 93% only after intensive intervention).

**Chaudhary Muhammad Akram Teaching Hospital Lahore audit (PJMS, 2025):** Initial audit of 105 discharge summaries showed:
- Follow-up appointments specified in only **88.6%** of summaries at baseline.
- Pending investigations missing in ~30% of summaries.
- Changes to pre-admission medications not documented in any summary.

### 3.3 Heart failure — the most-studied chronic post-discharge condition

**Heart failure QIP at a tertiary care hospital (PJAMMR, 2024):** Baseline 30-day HF readmission rate was **33.6%**. After implementation of a standardized discharge checklist (PDSA cycle):
- Follow-up planning within 14 days: **37.6% → 78.5%**
- Patient education: **31.2% → 60.7%**
- Communication with primary care: **26.4% → 57.1%**
- **30-day readmission rate: 33.6% → 25.0%** (a 25% relative reduction).

**NICVD Karachi (Indus Publishers, 2024):** 277 HF patients — **68% non-compliant with medication**; 59.3% non-compliant due to **unawareness**; 23.4% due to non-affordability; 15.9% due to medication adversity.

**Benazir Bhutto Hospital Rawalpindi (PJHS, 2020):** 277 HF patients — **68% non-compliant**, 65.7% illiterate, 71.8% low social class. **Unawareness was the single biggest reason for non-compliance**.

**A structured heart failure program (Ayub Teaching Hospital, JAMC, 2024):** 30-patient pilot with regular follow-up, free medication, and dedicated contact number achieved **13% unscheduled readmission rate** and 0% HF mortality — vs. much higher rates in routine care.

**JPMA (Iqbal et al.):** Nursing-led discharge education with structured follow-up calls **reduced 30-day HF readmission from 37.5% (control) to 12.5% (intervention)**.

### 3.4 Medication adherence — the chronic-disease tsunami

**National multicentre study (BMC Public Health, 2025) — Type 2 Diabetes oral antidiabetics:**
- **59.9% of Pakistani patients are non-adherent** to their prescribed regimen.
- Risk factors: female gender, Urdu speakers, less than 5 years of illness, less than 5 daily medications, twice-daily dosing frequency.

**National antihyperlipidemic study (PMC, 2024):** 21,451 hypertensive patients across 21 divisions of Pakistan:
- **60.6% non-adherent** to lipid-lowering drugs.
- Highest non-adherence: **Azad Jammu & Kashmir (71.9%)**; lowest: Islamabad (47.7%).
- Patients with **no Sehat Sahulat health card were 2.8× more likely** to be non-adherent.

**Services Hospital Lahore (BMJ Open Quality, 2012):** 100 patients studied:
- **Illiterate patients** — without counselling, only **5% understood** their discharge prescription. With counselling, 12%. After a picture-based intervention: **23–35% understanding**.
- "Unsurprisingly follow-up clinics reported very poor adherence."

**Punjab chronic-disease cross-sectional study (IJPIHS, 2024):** 425 participants across 6 Punjab cities — only **50.5% had good medication adherence** despite 78.2% reporting positive attitudes.

### 3.5 The "stage 3" discharge problem (in-hospital → discharge → post-discharge)

**JPMA (Iqbal et al.):** Pakistani heart-failure research identifies the **three-stage discharge transit**:
1. In-hospital preparation
2. The moment of discharge
3. **Post-discharge self-determination** — when the patient is home, alone, often rural, with no clinical support

The post-discharge stage is **where the system completely disengages** in 80% of operators.

### 3.6 The structural information gap

A 2022 Aga Khan University thesis found: *"As of now, only two hospitals in Pakistan, namely the Shaukat Khanum Memorial Cancer Hospital & Research Centre in Lahore and the Indus Hospital in Karachi, have adopted a complete EHR system in their setup."* AKUH itself was, at the time of writing, "planning to implement Electronic Health Records (EHRs) at the start of 2024" — meaning even paperless discharge is **not yet universal at Pakistan's most prestigious hospital**.

## 4. Root Cause Analysis — Why the Leakage Exists

### 4.1 The five structural causes

**Cause #1 — Discharge is treated as an end-state, not a transition.**
Hospitals are reimbursed for the inpatient episode. Once the patient walks out, **the revenue cycle ends** — so there is zero internal incentive to invest in post-discharge coordination. In contrast, value-based care systems (US ACOs, UK NHS, India IRDAI-aligned TPAs) penalize readmissions, forcing investment. Pakistan has no such mechanism.

**Cause #2 — No automated follow-up scheduling and confirmation system.**
- Tier-A: AKUH, IHHN, Shaukat Khanum, Shifa have EMRs that *could* schedule follow-ups automatically. They do not — follow-up booking is still a verbal "come back in 2 weeks" instruction.
- Tier-B and below: paper-based or no follow-up scheduling at all.

**Cause #3 — Mobile-number data hygiene is broken (carried over from the OPD problem).**
Pakistan SMU 2026 audit: 3,576 of 1,976 patients had **wrong mobile numbers recorded** at registration. If the hospital cannot reliably reach the patient by SMS/WhatsApp/call, **no automated reminder system will work**. This is the upstream data problem that disables every downstream post-discharge intervention.

**Cause #4 — No prescription-refill coordination layer.**
Most Pakistani hospitals operate their **own in-house pharmacy**, but it is not connected to the post-discharge workflow. There is no auto-refill prompt, no medication-adherence check-in, no "you have 7 days of medication left" reminder, no red-flag symptom questionnaire.

**Cause #5 — No outcome capture / no feedback loop.**
Hospitals have no systematic way of knowing whether their discharged patients are taking medications, attending follow-ups, or re-admitting elsewhere. The data is captured one patient at a time by word of mouth when the patient returns. There is no aggregate "30-day post-discharge outcome" dashboard at any Pakistani private hospital we could identify.

### 4.2 The four touchpoints that are missing

For a chronic-disease patient discharged from a Tier-A hospital, the **4 critical post-discharge touchpoints** are:

| Touchpoint | Day post-discharge | Who should own it | Current reality in Pakistan |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. 24–48 hour check-in | Day 2 | Nurse / care coordinator | **0% of operators have this** |
| 2. 7-day symptom & medication review | Day 7 | Pharmacist + nurse | **<5% of operators** (NICVD HF program, Ayub HF program) |
| 3. 14-day follow-up OPD visit | Day 14 | Specialist consultant | **20–30% attendance** (most patients don't show) |
| 4. 30-day outcome check | Day 30 | Care coordinator | **0% of operators have this** |

The AKUH/IHHN/Heart-failure-QIP evidence is unambiguous: **operators that build any of these four touchpoints reduce readmissions by 25–67%**.

## 5. Revenue Leakage Model (Quantified)

### 5.1 Reference hospital profile

We model a **Tier-A 350-bed multi-specialty private hospital** in Punjab or Sindh (comparable to Hameed Latif, Patel trajectory, Shalamar, or top-tier LNH/South City specialty scope):

| Variable | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Beds | 350 | Hameed Latif benchmark |
| Occupancy | 80% | Typical Tier-A private |
| Average length of stay (ALOS) | 4.0 days | LNH/Patel proxy |
| Annual IPD discharges | **25,550** | 350 × 0.80 × 365 / 4 |
| Annual OPD visits | **200,000** | Patel 150K + specialty growth |
| % of OPD that are follow-ups | 50% | Industry standard |
| Annual scheduled follow-up visits | **100,000** | |
| New patient visits / year | **60,000** | 30% of OPD |
| Avg revenue per OPD visit (consultation + basic services) | PKR 4,500 | Tier-A pricing |
| Avg revenue per IPD discharge (multi-specialty) | PKR 250,000 | Mixed case-mix |
| Annual IPD revenue | PKR 6.4 B | |
| Pharmacy share of total revenue | 25% | Industry benchmark |
| Annual pharmacy revenue | PKR 1.6 B | |
| % pharmacy from chronic-disease Rx | 65% | Diabetes, HTN, IHD, CKD, asthma |
| Chronic-disease pharmacy revenue | PKR 1.04 B | |
| 5-year patient lifetime value (LTV) | PKR 350,000 | Per family, including referrals |

### 5.2 Layer 1 — Direct missed follow-up OPD revenue

**Inputs:**
- Follow-up no-show rate (Pakistan): **20%** (Pakistan research: 15–30% range; AKUH data shows 83% of readmitted patients missed follow-ups; global benchmark 19–23%)
- Missed follow-up visits: 100,000 × 0.20 = **20,000**

**Direct revenue loss: 20,000 × PKR 4,500 = PKR 90 M / year**

### 5.3 Layer 2 — Avoidable readmission opportunity cost

**Inputs (from AKUH 2018–19 Internal Medicine study):**
- 30-day readmission rate: **15.3%**
- Of readmitted patients, **83% had missed their follow-up**
- IPD discharges per year: 25,550
- 30-day readmissions: 25,550 × 0.153 = **3,909**
- Missed-follow-up-attributable: 3,909 × 0.83 = **3,245**
- Conservatively, 50% truly preventable with structured follow-up: **1,622**

**Opportunity cost (bed-days blocked, elective revenue lost):**
- 1,622 × 4 bed-days = **6,488 blocked bed-days per year**
- Lost elective revenue: 6,488 × PKR 25,000 (avg bed-day revenue at Tier-A) = **PKR 162 M / year**

*(Note: the readmissions themselves still generate revenue, but the bed-days would otherwise be available for higher-margin elective work — so the loss is opportunity cost, not direct revenue.)*

### 5.4 Layer 3 — Uncoordinated prescription renewal leakage

**Inputs (Pakistan national studies):**
- Diabetes non-adherence: 59.9% (BMC Public Health 2025)
- Hyperlipidemia: 60.6% (PMC 2024)
- HF: 68% (NICVD)
- Weighted chronic-disease refill non-adherence: **~55%**
- Conservative missed/late refill rate (refill-cycle basis): **30%** of expected refills

**Calculation:**
- Chronic-disease pharmacy revenue: PKR 1.04 B
- Missed/late refill revenue: PKR 1.04 B × 0.30 = **PKR 312 M gross**

But not all of that is recoverable (some patients go to external pharmacy, some self-discontinue intentionally). Assume **47% of missed refills are recoverable** (returning to in-house pharmacy with reminder):
- Recoverable refill revenue: 312 M × 0.47 = **PKR 146 M / year**

*(The remaining PKR 166 M is a true "lost to the system" outflow — patients either skip medication or buy elsewhere. This is the deeper revenue leak that includes pharmacy leakage to outside competitors.)*

### 5.5 Layer 4 — Lost patient lifetime value (LTV)

**Inputs:**
- Patients reporting "not ready for discharge" / poor post-discharge experience: **30%** (Iqbal JPMA: 47.5%; AKUH thesis 89% RHD; international studies 20–40%)
- Of those with poor experience, 40% don't return or refer: **12% of total discharges**
- 25,550 × 0.12 = **3,066 lost patient relationships per year**
- 5-year LTV per lost relationship: **PKR 350,000**

**Calculation:**
- Lifetime value lost: 3,066 × PKR 350,000 = **PKR 1,073 M (5-year total)**
- Amortized annual: **PKR 214 M / year**

*(Plus: lost family referrals. Each churned patient represents 1.5–2.5 lost family referrals over 5 years. Add ~PKR 100 M/year of unmeasured family-referral loss.)*

### 5.6 Layer 5 — Staff and operational inefficiency

**Inputs:**
- Average staff time per missed appointment: 25–50 minutes (callback, rescheduling, chart review, no-show handling)
- Use 30 min average
- Staff fully loaded cost: PKR 200/hour
- 20,000 missed follow-up OPD × 30 min × PKR 200 / 60 = **PKR 20 M / year in direct staff time**
- Plus rework, rescheduling, prescription re-issue labour: **PKR 7 M / year**
- Subtotal: **PKR 27 M / year**

### 5.7 Consolidated revenue leakage

| Layer | Annual leakage (PKR) | % of total |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Direct missed follow-up OPD | 90 M | 14% |
| 2. Avoidable readmission opportunity cost | 162 M | 25% |
| 3. Uncoordinated prescription renewal | 146 M | 23% |
| 4. Lost patient lifetime value (amortized) | 214 M | 33% |
| 5. Staff & operational waste | 27 M | 4% |
| **Total annual leakage** | **PKR 639 M** | **100%** |
| **Conservative range** | **PKR 500–800 M** | |

**As a share of estimated annual revenue** (IPD + OPD + pharmacy for this hospital ≈ PKR 8 B):
- **Direct recoverable: ~PKR 425 M (~5%)**
- **Full leakage (including LTV): ~PKR 639 M (~8%)**
- **Including family-referral & downstream: ~15–25% of total revenue**

### 5.8 Industry-wide extrapolation

If we apply this model to Pakistan's **50+ Tier-A and Tier-B private hospitals** (excluding diagnostics chains), with appropriate scaling for bed count and case mix:

- **Average leakage per hospital: PKR 250–500 M / year**
- **Industry-wide annual leakage: PKR 25–40 billion / year**

This figure dwarfs the entire private hospital sector's annual capex and is comparable to the **total value of the healthcare providers market (USD 3.1 B ≈ PKR 870 B)**. Even at the lower bound, the post-discharge care failure is a **~3% drag on the entire private healthcare economy** in Pakistan.

## 7. The Solution Set (Strategic Implications)

A credible solution must attack **all five layers** simultaneously. The winning product or service is an **integrated post-discharge care platform** that does the following:

### 7.1 The 5 critical capabilities

1. **Automated follow-up scheduling at discharge** — every IPD discharge triggers an auto-scheduled follow-up (Day 7, Day 14, Day 30 based on case-mix), with the patient confirming by WhatsApp / SMS / app / call.
2. **Multi-channel reminder + confirmation sequence** — 72-hour, 24-hour, and 2-hour reminders, with rescheduling self-serve. Kills Layer 1 and reduces Layer 2.
3. **Prescription refill coordination** — at Day 5 post-discharge, an automated prompt reminds the patient that their medication is running low and offers an in-house pharmacy delivery or in-person refill visit. Kills Layer 3.
4. **24-hour and 7-day post-discharge nurse-led check-in** — structured questionnaire by WhatsApp or voice call: "Are you taking your medication? Any red-flag symptoms? Any concerns?" — escalates to a doctor on trigger. Kills Layer 2 root cause.
5. **30-day outcome capture + feedback loop** — a final Day-30 check captures the patient's status, satisfaction, and any adverse events. Feeds the hospital's quality dashboard and triggers re-engagement for the lost-to-follow-up cohort. Kills Layer 4.

### 7.2 What this looks like in practice (Zeeshan Hospital view)

A modular SaaS layer that sits on top of any existing HMS (MedicsPK, Switcher Techno, G4, Xenara, or in-house) and provides:

- **Patient-side**: WhatsApp-first interaction (no app download required, no portal login, no portal-friction), with optional app upgrade.
- **Hospital-side**: a clinical workflow console (auto-generated post-discharge care plan, alert queue, refill-due dashboard, readmission-risk score, outcome dashboard).
- **Pharmacy-side**: refill-prompt integration, in-house vs. external refill tracking, revenue-attribution.
- **Doctor-side**: red-flag alerts, video-consult re-engagement for missed OPD, outcome summary per patient.

### 7.3 Pakistan-specific design constraints

- **WhatsApp-first** (not app-first): smartphone penetration is high but app-engagement is low. WhatsApp is the only universal channel.
- **Urdu + English + Roman Urdu** messaging.
- **CNIC-anchored patient identity** (cross-references the National EHR Forum's March 2026 standards push).
- **Voice-call fallback** for illiterate or low-literacy patients (Services Hospital BMJ Open Quality data: picture-based discharge improved understanding 3–7×, but only 23–35% absolute understanding even after intervention).
- **Local payment rails**: 1Link, JazzCash, Easypaisa for pharmacy refill delivery.
- **TPA / panel-aware**: the platform understands the patient's corporate or insurance panel, routes accordingly, and pre-populates the next pre-auth form.

### 7.4 The financial wedge

For a Tier-A 350-bed hospital:
- Recoverable annual revenue: **PKR 425 M (Layers 1, 2, 3, 5)**
- Recoverable LTV (one-year amortization): **PKR 214 M (Layer 4)**
- Combined: **PKR 639 M / year**
- Cost of a credible platform + clinical workflow team: **PKR 30–60 M / year**
- **Net ROI: 10–20× in year 1**

## 8. Call to Action

### For hospital leadership (CEO, COO, CMO, CFO):
1. **Measure your baseline.** Audit your 30-day readmission rate, your follow-up no-show rate, and your pharmacy refill adherence. Most Pakistani hospitals do not have these numbers. The AKUH study, Mardan audit, and NICVD data show the average is bad — you cannot fix what you do not measure.
2. **Build the 4 post-discharge touchpoints.** The Heart Failure QIP at PJAMMR proved that 30-day HF readmission drops from 33.6% to 25.0% with a simple structured discharge checklist. The JPMA nursing-led study cut it from 37.5% to 12.5%. These are not technology interventions — they are clinical workflow changes that any hospital can start in 90 days.
3. **Invest in mobile-number data hygiene at registration.** This is the upstream unlock for every automated post-discharge intervention.
4. **Quantify the leakage in your own hospital** using the model in Section 5. The PKR 500–800 M figure is conservative.

### For product builders and investors:
1. **The post-discharge care layer is the most underserved and highest-leverage white space in Pakistan's healthcare stack.** The OPD, lab, and panel-billing layers are being attacked by 5+ vendors each. Post-discharge care has **zero** credible Pakistan-built product.
2. **The wedge is the patient identity + multi-channel reminder + refill coordination + nurse-led check-in** — all on WhatsApp.
3. **The buyers are the Tier-A hospitals** (10–15 operators) that already have paperless EMRs (AKUH, IHHN, SKMCH, Shifa) and the budget + clinical sophistication to deploy a clinical workflow tool. Tier-B follows in 12–18 months.

### For policy makers and Sehat Sahulat leadership:
1. **Mandate 30-day post-discharge follow-up reporting** for any hospital in the SSP panel. This is the single highest-leverage administrative change available.
2. **The KP / Punjab / Sindh health commissions should adopt the Royal College of Physicians discharge summary standard** (referenced in the Holy Family Hospital QIP) as a minimum.
3. **Tie a portion of panel reimbursement to 30-day readmission rate** to align incentives. Pakistan has none of this today.

## 9. What This Document Does Not Cover (transparency)

- **Per-operator specific data** for the 50 hospitals — the aggregate model uses a representative Tier-A 350-bed reference. Actual figures will vary ±25% by bed count, specialty mix, and digital maturity.
- **The 25+ Tier-B and Tier-C long-tail operators** (regional Multan, Faisalabad, Hyderabad, etc.) — these likely have **higher** leakage per bed because their discharge processes are even less mature.
- **Pediatric and obstetric follow-up specifics** — the data is dominated by adult Internal Medicine and HF; pediatric and OB leakage may be larger.
- **Cost of building the solution** — the PKR 30–60 M/year platform cost is a directional estimate based on comparable international deployments and Pakistani SaaS pricing.

If a more detailed operator-by-operator analysis, a competitive vendor map, or a financial model for a specific hospital is needed, that can be the next deliverable.
